# PLAY Heuristics (Desurvire & Wiberg, 2009)

The refined successor to HEP. Built from 116 candidate principles tested against
high- vs. low-rated games; the 48 that statistically separated good from bad games
are organized into three categories below. PLAY is **modular** — drop any heuristic
or whole category that doesn't apply (e.g., remove Game Story for a game with no
narrative). It is explicitly designed to be used from the concept phase onward, which
makes it the primary checklist for evaluating serious-game prototypes.

Rate each as Pass / Partial / Fail / N/A with a one-line evidence note and a severity
(0–4) for anything below Pass.

## I. Game Play

**Enduring Play**
- Fun, with no repetitive or boring tasks.
- Players are not penalized repeatedly for the same failure.
- Players do not lose hard-won progress/possessions.
- Play is long and sustains interest; fatigue/boredom minimized by varying activity and pace.

**Challenge, Strategy & Pace**
- Challenge, strategy, and pace are in balance.
- Paced to apply pressure without frustrating; difficulty rises as the player gains mastery.
- Easy to learn, hard to master.
- Challenges feel like positive experiences (want to continue), not negative ones (want to quit).
- Any AI/opposition is balanced and forces varied tactics.

**Consistency in Game World**
- The world reacts to the player and "remembers" their passage.
- Changes the player makes are persistent and noticeable.

**Goals**
- Goals are clear: an overriding goal presented EARLY, plus short-term goals throughout.
- Skills needed for a goal are taught early enough to use, or just before they're needed.
- Rewards deepen engagement by increasing capability, capacity, or customization.

**Variety of Players & Game Styles**
- Supports a variety of play styles.
- Balanced with multiple ways to win.
- The first ~10 minutes of play are obvious and give immediate positive feedback to all player types.
- Difficulty accommodates novices through experts.

**Players' Perception of Control**
- Players feel in control and sense their influence on the game world.

## II. Coolness / Entertainment / Humor / Emotional Immersion

**Emotional Connection** — there is an emotional connection between the player and the
game world and their avatar/role.
**Coolness / Entertainment** — the game offers something distinct that attracts and retains interest.
**Humor** — humor is used well (where appropriate to the subject).
**Immersion** — visceral, audio, and visual content deepen immersion.

## III. Usability & Game Mechanics

**Documentation / Tutorial**
- Playable without reading a manual.
- Playable without needing to open the tutorial first (learn by doing).

**Status & Score**
- Controls are consistent and follow conventions.
- Status/score indicators are seamless, obvious, available, and don't obstruct play.
- Controls are intuitive, naturally mapped, customizable, defaulting to standard settings.
- Consistency with genre conventions shortens the learning curve.

**Game Provides Feedback**
- Reacts in a consistent, immediate, challenging, and exciting way to player actions.
- Provides appropriate audio / visual / visceral feedback.

**Terminology** — clear, consistent language; goals and skill cues are legible.

**Burden on Player**
- No unnecessary burden / cognitive load.
- Controls basic enough to learn fast, expandable for advanced players.

**Screen Layout**
- Efficient, integrated, visually pleasing.
- Consistent UI (color, type, dialogue, controls).
- HUD feels like part of the game; art is recognizable and signals its function.

**Navigation** — consistent, logical, minimalist.

**Error Prevention**
- Player error is avoided.
- Interruption is supported: easy to stop/resume and save in different states.
- On launch, the player has enough info to begin.
- Context-sensitive help so players aren't stuck and forced to a manual.
- All skill levels can get involved quickly via tutorials and/or adjustable difficulty.

**Game Story Immersion** — the story encourages immersion (omit if no story component).

## Serious-game application notes

- **Goals** and **Documentation/Tutorial** are the two most commonly failed heuristics
  in serious games: designers front-load rules as text and forget to state the win
  condition. Watch for both.
- **Multiple ways to win** is a procedural-rhetoric tell: if there's only one optimal
  path, the game is a quiz, not a decision space — and a dominant strategy may teach
  the wrong lesson (cross-check with procedural-rhetoric.md).
- **Emotional Connection** is where an avatar/role and stakes live; it's often a
  serious game's greatest strength and worth naming explicitly.
