# Motivation: Self-Determination Theory in Games (Ryan, Rigby & Przybylski, 2006)

SDT explains *why* games engage and sustain play. Three innate needs predict
enjoyment, persistence, and post-play wellbeing. For a serious game, engagement is
not optional polish — if players disengage, no learning happens. Rate each need
High / Medium / Low with evidence.

## The three needs

- **Autonomy** — the player acts from genuine volition and meaningful choice. In a
  serious game this means real decisions with real tradeoffs, not a single sanctioned
  "correct" answer dressed as a choice. (Directly tied to PLAY "multiple ways to win"
  and to the procedural-rhetoric "no real choice" failure.)
- **Competence** — the player feels effective and is improving. Requires immediate,
  clear, fair feedback on actions and a learnable difficulty curve. Silent meter
  changes, opaque scoring, and unexplained outcomes starve competence.
- **Relatedness** — connection to others or to characters/roles/stakes. Carried by an
  avatar/role, NPCs who react, social/leaderboard elements, or emotionally weighted
  consequences.

## How to apply

For each need, point to the specific mechanic that supports or starves it, and connect
to fixes already surfaced elsewhere in the evaluation (e.g., "Competence is Low because
choices change resources with no visible feedback — see the Feedback heuristic Fail and
the Tier-1 'consequence banner' asset").

## Evidence base for stakeholder buy-in

Systematic reviews of serious gaming and gamification in health-professions and
medical education find these games are **at least as effective** as conventional or
other digital methods for knowledge and skills, and can improve satisfaction — but the
evidence is mostly low-to-moderate quality and **outcomes depend heavily on design
quality**. Use this two-handed: it justifies investing in a game *and* justifies
investing in getting the design right (which is what this evaluation is for). Cite
specifics from references/sources.md when writing for a skeptical audience.
