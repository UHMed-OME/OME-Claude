# HEP — Heuristic Evaluation for Playability (Desurvire, Caplan & Toth, 2004)

The original game-playability heuristic set (CHI 2004), adapted from productivity-
software usability heuristics. ~43 heuristics in four categories. Most useful for
broad coverage and for early prototype/mock-up evaluation. PLAY (play.md) refines
this; use HEP as a complementary lens, especially its Game Story category.

Rate Pass / Partial / Fail / N/A + note + severity (0–4).

## Game Play
- Provide clear goals — an overriding goal presented early, plus short-term goals.
- The game is fair; outcomes are perceived as the player's own doing.
- Reward the player; rewards are meaningful and increase capability.
- Pace to apply pressure but not frustrate; difficulty ramps with skill.
- A good challenge/skill balance (flow): easy to learn, hard to master.
- The first player action is intuitive and gives immediate, positive feedback.
- Actions/mechanics have consistent, meaningful, predictable results.
- Players don't lose hard-won progress; failure is not punitively repetitive.

## Game Story
- Players grasp the storyline as a single, consistent vision.
- The story/fantasy is interesting and pulls the player in.
- The game transports the player into a level of personal, emotional, visceral involvement.
- The player invests in possible story outcomes (cares what happens).

## Game Mechanics
- The system reacts consistently, challengingly, and excitingly to player actions.
- Controls are intuitive and naturally mapped.
- Mechanics have the correct "weight," responsiveness, and timing.

## Game Usability
- Provide consistent responses to player actions.
- Allow customization of video/audio/difficulty.
- Controls are intuitive; minimize unnecessary control options.
- Immediate feedback on player actions.
- Players should not need the manual to play.
- Status indicators are visible and do not obstruct play.
- Navigation is consistent and intuitive.

## How HEP and PLAY relate
HEP is the broader, older taxonomy; PLAY is the empirically filtered, concept-phase
set. Where they overlap (goals, feedback, tutorial-free start), a Fail on both is a
strong, well-supported finding. HEP's distinct value here is **Game Story**, which
PLAY compresses into a single Immersion item — for narrative/empathy serious games,
score Game Story from HEP in full.
